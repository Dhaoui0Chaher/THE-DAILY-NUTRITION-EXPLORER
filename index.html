Scientific basis, calculations & limits

Visible methodology rather than hidden assumptions.

1. Daily reference intakes (DRI)

Age- and sex-category reference values are based on the U.S./Canadian Dietary Reference Intakes (Health Canada tables, updated November 2025). Adult energy estimation uses the published Mifflin–St Jeor resting-energy equation, multiplied by a conventional activity factor. We show energy only for nonpregnant, nonlactating adults age 19–78 with weight and height; beyond that validation age range, energy is deliberately hidden. Calorie estimation is not suitable for all people.

2. Food portions and arithmetic

Each example has approximate nutrient composition per 100 g edible food. If 100 g of pepper contains an estimated 127.7 mg of vitamin C, a 120 g pepper has ~153 mg: 127.7 × 120 / 100. The mass equivalent to one day's reference is 100 × target ÷ nutrient-per-100g. Values and serving sizes are approximate and not individual food-lot lab tests. Known plate quantities are calculated additively. Missing nutrient fields are unknown (not zero); a lower-bound known subtotal is flagged partial.

3. Nutrient definitions matter

  • Vitamin A is expressed as µg RAE, folate as µg DFE, and niacin as mg NE. Example food figures for these are approximations; some B3 food entries represent preformed niacin and therefore can undercount niacin equivalents contributed by tryptophan.
  • Vitamin D amounts use µg, not international units; 1 µg is 40 IU.
  • Omega-3 is ALA; marine EPA/DHA are distinct and do not have an official U.S. RDA shown here.
  • Protein is an RDA (0.8 g/kg for healthy adults), or the DRI age-group value if no valid body weight is entered. Carbohydrates show the minimum RDA, not a total diet target.
  • There is no universal RDA for total fat. When an adult energy estimate is available, this app shows a 20–35% calorie range, with the midpoint only for overview context. Sodium 1,500 mg is an AI for many adults, not a recommendation to add salt; the adult chronic disease risk reduction level is 2,300 mg/day. Nutrient reference intakes are not toxicological upper limits.
  • Water AI is total water from foods and beverages; actual requirements change with heat, exertion, medical conditions, and losses.

3b. Nine indispensable amino acids and uncertain trace requirements

For nonpregnant adults age 19+, the app displays weight-based FAO/WHO/UNU (2007) average indispensable-amino-acid requirements (mg/kg/day), rather than a personalized RDA. They are not appropriate targets for children or pregnancy. Individual food amino-acid profiles have not been measured in this demo, so meal totals are marked unmeasured—not inferred from protein grams. Food chromium AI is retained as a historical North American DRI; newer evidence questions its essentiality. Boron, nickel, silicon, vanadium, sulfate and dietary cobalt are displayed as no established independent intake target, rather than inventing needs. EPA/DHA have no U.S. RDA; an absence of an RDA does not imply a lack of physiological relevance.

4. Dataset provenance and quality

The self-contained starter food library and composite recipes contain illustrative, rounded, generic food-composition estimates assembled for this demonstrator, with common edible portion weights. It is not a verified row-by-row USDA FoodData Central export. Some special trace-nutrient amounts are informed by the NIH ODS selected-food tables, but are still approximate because of variability. Missing iodine, chromium, fluoride, biotin, and molybdenum measurements for many foods must not be assumed to be zero. Before clinical use or publication, replace each record with a documented USDA FDC ID, analytical data type, preparation method, and publication date; consider national food-composition data for Tunisian products.

5. Weekly meal plans and purchase conversions

The weekly planner creates seven breakfasts, lunches, dinners and snacks. The selected protein-main frequency applies to 14 lunch/dinner mains, and other components are meat- and fish-free. Swapping one main may change the frequency count. Groceries aggregate grams across every named ingredient, then multiply cooked/edible recipe weights by editable illustrative buy-weight factors. This is the Food Buying Guide's general purchase-yield principle, not a set of verified USDA yield factors for these exact dishes. Boiled pulses and grains are converted to dry weights; cooked meat to approximate boneless raw weight; produce may be adjusted for trimmings and peels. Water is excluded. Additional seasoning, salt, stock, and any ingredients not explicitly named in a recipe are not included. These menus do not optimize all daily nutrient reference values and may be inappropriate for children, pregnancy, lactation, allergies or medical diets.

6. What this prototype does not claim to do

It cannot infer a person's deficiencies, diagnose disease, guarantee micronutrient adequacy, account for bioavailability or drug–nutrient interactions, determine tolerable upper intake levels for all nutrients, prescribe supplement doses, or model essential amino-acid requirements and all biologically useful food compounds. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, sports nutrition, chronic illnesses, and special diets require tailored advice. Mineral values can change with soil and water; iodine in particular varies widely. Do not use food equivalents as a suggestion to eat excessive amounts of a single food, especially liver, oils, or highly salted foods.
